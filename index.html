Do strong ball-strikers with cold putters become elite?

PGA Tour player-seasons with 10+ strokes-gained events, 2015–2020 vs. the following season

Bennett Wicker

Yes. Players who were not yet elite, ranked in the top 15% for ball-striking and putted below tour average became elite the next season at nearly four times the rate of other non-elite players: … (…) against … (…), or …×. Ball-striking repeats from year to year and putting repeats less than ball-striking, so a cold putter on a strong ball-striker is usually temporary. Recommendation: build your signing shortlist from candidates; it takes about … candidate signings per breakout (…) against about … otherwise (…).

Premise: sponsor value is concentrated in elite players, so the goal is to find future elite players early. The data has no sales or contract figures, so this report measures who becomes elite, not what they are worth.

The scorecard

Season t = 2015–2020, … player-seasons. Each rate shows its count, because the groups are small.

Elite: a top-10 finish in at least 30% of starts. Candidate: not elite, ball-striking (off the tee + approach, per round) above the 85th percentile of qualified players that season, and putting below tour average (strokes gained putting < 0). Breakout: not elite in season t, elite in season t+1. A player with no qualifying next season counts as not elite.

Data notes

Source

  • PGA Tour Golf Data (2015-2022) on Kaggle by Rob Mulla, originally from Advanced Sports Analytics. License: CC0 (public domain).
  • Every player is a professional tour golfer whose results are public. The data holds no other personal information.
  • The raw file, pga_raw.csv, has 36,864 rows and 37 columns: 499 players, 333 tournaments, seasons 2015–2022.

What one row is

  • Raw file: one player in one tournament.
  • Analysis file (players.csv, built by prep_data.py): one player in one season with at least 10 events that have strokes-gained data. Both pages read this file and compute every number in the browser.

How the data was cleaned

  • Kept 18 of the 37 raw columns; dropped the fantasy-points columns, date (known errors; season is the time column) and three empty columns.
  • Dropped 42 rows (21 player id + tournament id pairs shared by two same-name golfers).
  • Dropped the partial 2022 season (3,674 rows), leaving 33,148 event rows for 2015–2021, 26,032 of them with strokes gained.
  • Kept player-seasons with at least 10 strokes-gained events: … rows, … per season.
  • Filled missing pos from Finish only when made_cut = 1 and Finish is a clean top 10 (1, 2, T8 and so on): 28 of 30 such rows counted, mostly the fall-2020 Shriners and Sanderson Farms. The 2 not counted are 2015–2016 Northern Trust rows with made_cut = 0, where Finish is unreliable. This makes Abraham Ancer and Patrick Cantlay elite in 2021 and moves the other non-elite breakout rate to 2.8% (26 of 941), from 2.6% (24 of 941) without the rule.

How each summary number is computed

All four use rows with season t = 2015–2020.

  • Candidate breakout rate = candidate rows elite the next season ÷ all candidate rows.
  • Other non-elite breakout rate = non-elite, non-candidate rows elite the next season ÷ all such rows.
  • Elite stay-elite rate = elite rows elite the next season ÷ all elite rows.
  • Ball-striking year-to-year correlation = Pearson correlation of ball-striking in season t and t+1, for rows with a qualified next season.
  • Signings per breakout = 1 ÷ breakout rate. Break-even cost ratio = other non-elite rate ÷ candidate rate.

Caveats

  • Majors mostly lack strokes-gained data, so skill comes from other events while results include majors.
  • 2020 was shortened by COVID. 2021 contains two Masters and two U.S. Opens because the 2020 events moved into the fall.
  • The groups are small: the candidate rate rests on … breakouts in … player-seasons.
  • A second golfer with the same name could sit under a tour player's ID in events the tour player skipped.
  • Five 2016–2017 CareerBuilder Challenge rows have pos ≤ 10 (Hudson Swafford's 2017 win among them) but made_cut = 0 and 2 rounds; pos is kept and the other two fields are treated as errors. These rows have no strokes-gained data, so skill is unaffected.
  • Correlation is not proof of cause.
  • Finding 5 uses season t = 2015–2019 because it needs three consecutive seasons.
