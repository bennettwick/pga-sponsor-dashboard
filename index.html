Do strong ball-strikers with cold putters become elite?

PGA Tour player-seasons with 10+ strokes-gained events, 2015–2020 vs. the following season

Premise

Sponsor value is concentrated in elite players, so the goal is to find future elite players early. The data has no sales or contract figures, so this report measures who becomes elite, not what a player is worth.

The scorecard

Seasons 2015–2020, … player-seasons. Each rate shows its count, because the groups are small.

Definitions

Key terms

Elite
A top-10 finish in 30% or more of a player's starts. (A start is one tournament played.)
Candidate
A player who meets all three:
  • Is not elite that season
  • Ranks in the top 15% of qualified players that season for ball-striking (shots off the tee and into the green, per round)
  • Putts below the tour average (strokes gained putting under 0)
Breakout
Not elite in one season (t) and elite the next season (t+1).
No qualifying next season
The player did not play enough the next season to be counted (fewer than 10 events with strokes-gained data).
  • Counted as not elite and kept in the data, never dropped.

Data terms

Player-season
One player's results for one season. Every count on this site is a number of player-seasons.
Strokes gained
How many shots better (+) or worse (−) than the average tour player, per round.
Qualified player-season
At least 10 skill events.
Skill events
Events in the season with strokes-gained data.
Starts
All events played in the season, including majors and events without strokes gained.
Top-10 rate
Top-10 finishes ÷ starts. Missed cuts, withdrawals and disqualifications do not count as top 10s.
Per-round strokes gained
Each category averaged over skill events, weighted by rounds played.
Ball-striking
Strokes gained off the tee + approach, per round.
Ball-striking percentile
Rank of ball-striking among qualified players in the same season (0–1).
Below-average putting
Strokes gained putting < 0 per round (0 = tour average).

Appendix: data notes source, rows dropped, cleaning, formulas, caveats

Source

  • PGA Tour Golf Data (2015-2022) on Kaggle by Rob Mulla, originally from Advanced Sports Analytics. License: CC0 (public domain).
  • Every player is a professional tour golfer whose results are public. The data holds no other personal information.
  • The raw file, pga_raw.csv, has 36,864 rows and 37 columns: 499 players, 333 tournaments, seasons 2015–2022.

What one row is

  • Raw file: one player in one tournament.
  • Analysis file (players.csv, built by prep_data.py): one player in one season with at least 10 events that have strokes-gained data. Both pages read this file and compute every number in the browser.

How the data was cleaned

  • Kept 18 of the 37 raw columns; dropped the fantasy-points columns, date (known errors; season is the time column) and three empty columns.
  • Dropped 42 rows (21 player id + tournament id pairs shared by two same-name golfers).
  • Dropped the partial 2022 season (3,674 rows), leaving 33,148 event rows for 2015–2021, 26,032 of them with strokes gained.
  • Kept player-seasons with at least 10 strokes-gained events: … rows, … per season.
  • Filled missing pos from Finish only when made_cut = 1 and Finish is a clean top 10 (1, 2, T8 and so on): 28 of 30 such rows counted, mostly the fall-2020 Shriners and Sanderson Farms. The 2 not counted are 2015–2016 Northern Trust rows with made_cut = 0, where Finish is unreliable. Missed cuts, withdrawals, disqualifications and MDF results (made the cut but did not finish) are not top 10s. This makes Abraham Ancer and Patrick Cantlay elite in 2021 and moves the other non-elite breakout rate to 2.8% (26 of 941), from 2.6% (24 of 941) without the rule.

How each summary number is computed

All four use rows with season t = 2015–2020. Terms are defined in the Definitions section above.

  • Candidate breakout rate = candidate rows elite the next season ÷ all candidate rows.
  • Other non-elite breakout rate = non-elite, non-candidate rows elite the next season ÷ all such rows.
  • Elite stay-elite rate = elite rows elite the next season ÷ all elite rows.
  • Ball-striking year-to-year correlation = Pearson correlation of ball-striking in season t and t+1, for rows with a qualified next season.
  • Signings per breakout = 1 ÷ breakout rate. Break-even cost ratio = other non-elite rate ÷ candidate rate.

Caveats

  • Majors mostly lack strokes-gained data, so skill comes from other events while results include majors.
  • 2020 was shortened by COVID. 2021 contains two Masters and two U.S. Opens because the 2020 events moved into the fall.
  • The groups are small: the candidate rate rests on … breakouts in … player-seasons.
  • A second golfer with the same name could sit under a tour player's ID in events the tour player skipped.
  • Five 2016–2017 CareerBuilder Challenge rows have pos ≤ 10 (Hudson Swafford's 2017 win among them) but made_cut = 0 and 2 rounds; pos is kept and the other two fields are treated as errors. These rows have no strokes-gained data, so skill is unaffected.
  • The cold-putting condition does not by itself raise the breakout rate: with it off, the candidate rate is … (…).
  • Strong ball-strikers who putted at or above the tour average started closer to the elite line (average top-10 rate … vs. … for candidates), and their average top-10 rate the next season was about the same (… vs. …, among players with a qualified next season), so part of their higher breakout rate reflects where they started.
  • The candidate advantage appears only in 2018–2020: in 2015–2017, candidates broke out … against … for other non-elite players. The data cannot say why.
  • Correlation is not proof of cause.
  • The fifth finding uses seasons 2015–2019 because it needs three consecutive seasons.
